# Audience testing

Real, external reactions to the finished film, gathered after assembly -
not a formal user-research protocol, but genuine reactions from two
different audiences: the broad public, and the one person whose actual
memory the story is drawn from.

## Closest possible ground-truth check: the subject of the memory herself

The story is drawn from a real childhood memory involving the author's
mother. She was shown the finished film directly and responded:

> "I love it son, it's wonderful memory, and I'm still bargaining haha."

This is the single most meaningful piece of audience feedback this project
could get, for two reasons:
- **It confirms narrative accuracy** - she recognized the story as the real
  memory it's based on, rather than reading as a generic or distorted
  version of it (the one person in a position to judge that at all).
- **It confirms the tone landed** - "wonderful memory" plus "still
  bargaining haha" shows the intended register (warm, a little
  self-deprecating, not actually embarrassing in hindsight) came through,
  matching the film's own closing line ("being ashamed... is not even
  kindness") rather than landing as mockery of her.

## What this is and isn't

This is real, unprompted qualitative feedback from two genuine audiences
(general public via YouTube, and direct personal validation from the
story's subject) - not a structured usability test with a written protocol,
a sample size, or neutral/negative responses actively solicited. If there's
time before resubmission, a stronger version of this section would add: 2-3
more viewers (ideally outside the family) asked a specific question each -
"did the ending make sense without sound/visual context for the price
negotiation?", "was the Amharic pacing readable alongside the English
subtitles?" - rather than relying on unprompted reactions alone.
